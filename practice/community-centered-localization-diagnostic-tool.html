Community-Centered Localization Diagnostic Tool

From Questions of Power, Practice, and Accountability to Locally-Led Systems Change

The Challenge

Localization has become an increasingly prominent commitment across humanitarian aid, international development, philanthropy, and civil society. Organizations are being asked to shift resources, leadership, decision-making, and implementation closer to local actors.

Yet the language of localization can obscure an important question:

A central question

What is actually changing?

An organization may increase the number of local partners it works with without substantially shifting who controls resources. A project may be locally implemented while priorities continue to be determined elsewhere. Communities may participate in consultations while remaining largely absent from decisions about strategy, funding, program design, or accountability.

As a result, measuring localization through organizational policies, partnership numbers, or funding percentages alone can provide an incomplete picture.

The Community-Centered Localization Diagnostic Tool emerged from a need for a more reflective approach—one that examines localization not simply as an institutional commitment, but as a set of relationships involving power, decision-making, resources, accountability, partnership quality, and community voice.

The Initiative

Pathways Consulting LLC is developing the Community-Centered Localization Diagnostic Tool (CLDT) as a practical framework to help organizations examine how localization shows up across their existing systems and ways of working.

Rather than functioning as a compliance scorecard, the tool is designed to support structured organizational reflection.

The diagnostic asks

To what extent do an organization's systems, relationships, and decision-making practices create the conditions for genuinely locally led and community-centered work?

The diagnostic examines eight interconnected domains of organizational practice, bringing attention to questions such as:

  • Who defines priorities?
  • Who participates in strategic and programmatic decisions?
  • Who controls financial resources?
  • How are local partners positioned within institutional relationships?
  • Whose knowledge is treated as expertise?
  • How does community voice influence decisions?
  • Where does accountability ultimately sit?
  • What organizational systems reinforce—or constrain— locally led practice?

The framework is intentionally designed to move beyond a binary assessment of whether an organization “does localization.”

Instead, it asks organizations to examine where power currently sits, what practices are already changing, where contradictions remain, and what realistic pathways toward deeper transformation might look like.

The Approach

The tool is being designed around several principles intended to keep the diagnostic grounded in organizational reality rather than abstract commitments.

Start with existing evidence

Organizations should not need to generate extensive new documentation simply to participate in the diagnostic. Policies, partnership agreements, strategies, budgets, monitoring systems, organizational structures, and existing program information can provide an initial evidence base.

Examine practice, not only policy

An organization may have strong commitments on paper while everyday practices tell a more complicated story. The diagnostic therefore encourages users to examine both formal systems and how decisions are actually made.

Treat localization as multidimensional

Funding is important, but resource transfer alone does not determine whether work is locally led. Decision-making authority, knowledge, governance, partnership dynamics, accountability, and community influence must also be considered.

Surface tensions rather than hide them

Localization frequently involves competing incentives and institutional constraints. The tool is intended to make these tensions visible so organizations can engage with them rather than produce artificially positive assessments.

Create pathways for change

The purpose of diagnosis is not simply to identify weaknesses. Each domain is intended to help organizations recognize existing strengths, identify gaps, and define practical next steps.

Extending the Question to Communities

An important element of the initiative is the recognition that an organizational self-assessment cannot, by itself, determine whether work is genuinely community-centered.

An organization may consider its approach participatory while communities experience the relationship very differently.

For this reason, the broader concept includes development of a complementary community-facing or “reverse” diagnostic.

Instead of asking organizations how well they believe they share power, the reverse diagnostic would explore questions from the perspective of communities and local stakeholders:

  • Do people understand how decisions are made?
  • Can they influence priorities?
  • Do they know where resources are going?
  • Can they challenge decisions?
  • Do feedback mechanisms produce meaningful changes?
  • Do communities experience themselves as participants, partners, or decision-makers?

The comparison between institutional perception and community experience may reveal some of the most important insights.

Where the Work Is Going

The Community-Centered Localization Diagnostic Tool is currently being developed as an iterative framework rather than presented as a finished methodology.

The next phase is intended to test the tool with a small number of organizations, learn how the domains and questions function in real organizational settings, and refine both the organizational and community-facing components.

The longer-term objective is not to create another localization ranking.

It is to create a structured space for organizations to ask a harder question:

If we say we are committed to locally led development, what would we expect to see differently in the way power, resources, relationships, knowledge, and decisions move through our organization?

That question is where meaningful localization begins.
