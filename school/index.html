Pathways Development Practice School

Where development concepts become practice.

A new learning platform for practitioners working across development, humanitarian action, civil society, policy, and social change.

Currently in development · Launching in 2027

A school for the practice behind the principles.

The Pathways Development Practice School is an emerging learning platform designed to translate complex ideas about development, humanitarian action, localization, power, participation, and systems change into practical learning for people doing the work.

Development and humanitarian practice are filled with powerful concepts: locally led development, participation, equitable partnerships, community engagement, systems thinking, accountability, and localization.

But understanding the language is different from knowing what those ideas require in practice.

The Practice School is being developed to help bridge that gap.

Through applied learning, reflection, case-based discussion, practical tools, and facilitated exploration, the School will create space for practitioners to examine not only what good practice should look like, but also the systems, relationships, incentives, and power dynamics that shape what becomes possible.

The goal is not simply to teach development concepts. It is to create space for practitioners to examine how those concepts behave when they encounter real institutions, real constraints, real relationships, and real communities.

Learning that starts with harder questions.

The Practice School will encourage practitioners to move beyond technical answers and examine the systems that surround development and humanitarian decisions.

Power

Who gets to decide?

Examining where authority sits, whose priorities shape decisions, and how formal and informal power operate within development systems.

Knowledge

Whose expertise counts?

Exploring how institutional knowledge, professional expertise, lived experience, and community knowledge are valued differently within decision-making.

Resources

Who controls what becomes possible?

Understanding how financing, donor requirements, organizational incentives, and resource control influence priorities and available choices.

Participation

Is participation the same as influence?

Looking beyond consultation and representation to examine whether communities and local actors have meaningful influence over decisions.

Systems

What surrounds the problem?

Using systems thinking to examine relationships, incentives, constraints, feedback, unintended consequences, and interdependence.

Accountability

Who is accountable to whom?

Exploring how accountability moves between communities, organizations, donors, governments, partners, and institutional systems.

Emerging Learning Areas

The School is currently in development. Initial learning areas are expected to draw from the core questions and practice areas that shape Pathways Consulting's work.

Localization

Localization & Locally Led Development

Moving beyond localization language to examine power, resources, authority, partnership, accountability, and community influence in practice.

Systems

Systems Thinking & Power Analysis

Understanding how relationships, incentives, institutions, funding, and power shape complex development and humanitarian environments.

Communities

Community-Centered Practice

Exploring what changes when community knowledge, experience, priorities, and decision-making power are treated as central to development practice.

Partnerships

Equitable Partnerships

Examining partnership beyond organizational roles to understand how trust, resources, decision-making, accountability, and institutional power shape relationships.

Leadership

Humanitarian Leadership & Complexity

Exploring leadership within interconnected crises, uncertainty, institutional constraints, and changing systems of humanitarian action.

Learning

Facilitation & Organizational Learning

Using reflection, dialogue, participatory methods, systems tools, and facilitated sense-making to help organizations learn from experience and adapt practice.

Designed for applied learning.

The Practice School is being designed around a learning philosophy that connects ideas to the realities practitioners encounter in organizations, partnerships, communities, and complex systems.

Practice-Grounded

Start with real problems

Learning will be grounded in the tensions, choices, trade-offs, and uncertainties practitioners actually encounter rather than idealized models alone.

Reflective

Question assumptions

Participants will be encouraged to examine the assumptions, power dynamics, incentives, and institutional structures shaping their own practice.

Applied

Leave with something usable

Learning experiences will aim to connect reflection with practical tools, questions, frameworks, and approaches participants can take back into their own work.

As the School develops, learning formats may include:

Short Courses Applied Workshops Case-Based Learning Practitioner Tools Reflective Exercises Facilitated Learning Cohort-Based Learning
